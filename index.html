00

Research Question

Outcome-level rewards supervise the final result, but do not directly reveal which agent or which tokens contributed to the outcome. I investigate whether the model’s own attention can expose this hidden structure.

01

Observation

Executor generally tracks the Plan.

All four correctness states show a visible diagonal tracking structure. Even unsuccessful executions do not simply ignore the Planner; failure can arise despite substantial Plan-following behavior.

Alignment

A Diagonal Score measures how closely attention mass follows an idealized Plan-to-Executor diagonal. Planner correctness alone and Executor correctness alone are not sufficient to explain this pattern.

Tracking Dynamics

Centroid and Peak Tracking Error quantify how far the attention focus drifts from the expected execution position. The mismatch conditions TF and FT show larger deviations than TT and FF.

02

From Attention Dynamics to Cross-Agent Token Influence

The attention analysis raises a finer-grained question: which Planner tokens does the Executor actually depend on?

Prior inspiration

This project is inspired by recent work showing that attention dynamics can expose reasoning-critical tokens and support fine-grained policy optimization. Attention Illuminates LLM Reasoning.

The present project studies a different setting: cross-agent attention in a Planner–Executor architecture, with a different head-selection criterion and token-scoring procedure.

Do Attention-Selected Tokens Actually Matter?

03A

Influence Is Not the Same as Usefulness

The same intervention reveals a second, more subtle pattern: highly attended tokens can be either helpful or harmful.

7.98%

High attention but harmful

Among the top-attention group, some plans are actively harmful even though the Executor strongly depends on them.

14.02%

Low attention but helpful

Some helpful Plan information is not among the most strongly attended tokens.

“Cross-agent attention captures dependency, but dependency does not necessarily imply positive contribution.”

Preliminary takeaway: The attention signal appears more promising as a token-level credit modulation signal than as a direct agent-level reward allocation rule.
05

The Planner–Executor Bottleneck

The intervention studies suggest that Plan quality is a major bottleneck. A useful Plan can substantially improve the Executor, while a noisy Plan can be actively harmful.

No Plan65.28%
Normal Plan64.20%
Oracle Plan81.58%
Random Plan21.30%

Large oracle headroom

The difference between no-plan and oracle-plan settings indicates substantial headroom for better planning and better inter-agent communication.

Noisy plans can hurt

The normal Planner currently underperforms the no-plan condition, suggesting that the interface is valuable only when the transmitted information is reliable.
